# Practices — how to work the method (non-normative)

Short guides to **working practice**: the order to do things in, what a step costs, and what it
buys. One file per practice.

**These files define nothing.** The Dictum standard's text is the sole statement of the rules and
stands on its own — a reader can conform without anything here. A practice is what an experienced
operator would tell you *after* you had read the standard: not a bar, but the way the bars are
usually met without pain.

## How a practice differs from its neighbours

| | Holds | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** (`dictum`) | the rule, plus one clause of why | must an author follow it to conform? |
| **Study** (`../studies`) | a question, a method, and what the evidence showed | did we measure this? |
| **Practice** (here) | the recommended way of working, and its cost | would an experienced operator advise it? |
| **Skill** (`dictum/skills`) | the procedure an agent executes | is this an instruction to a tool? |

A practice may cite a study for its evidence and a skill for its mechanics. It never states a bar.
Where a practice and the standard's text disagree, the text wins.

## Conventions, per this repo's charter

- **Describe by shape, never by name** — no real product, repo, company, or model appears. Archetypes
  only ("a static-analysis CLI", "a self-hosted web app").
- **Non-normative**, and say so in front-matter.
- **Stable anchors** — every practice carries an `anchor: LAB-PRACTICE-*` listed in `../ANCHORS.md`,
  so a citation survives a rename.
- **Cost is stated, not implied.** A practice that never says what it costs is advocacy, not advice.
